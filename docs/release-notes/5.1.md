# PhoneKeep 5.1

**Verified on a Mac (Apple Silicon) with real iPhones. The Windows check is deferred:** the Windows installer is built and every automated test runs on Windows on each change, but no on-device Windows check was done for this release; it follows separately.

PhoneKeep is now **free software under the GNU GPL v3** (or any later version). The source is at github.com/aminyoutb007-hash/PhoneKeep. The licence and the list of libraries PhoneKeep is built on are inside the app.

## What's new

- **Check-up**: one screen with four cards (Storage, Battery, Stability, Security), each green, amber or red with what to do. *Why is my phone slow?* names the likeliest reasons first. A red card notifies you once, not every day.
  - **Storage**: the biggest things to clear first, each with the steps on the phone, and a forecast of when it fills up.
  - **Battery**: battery health over time, from your backups, with notes and tips for your model. *Save details* as a spreadsheet.
  - **Stability**: apps that crashed in the last 7 days, and restarts of the phone. PhoneKeep only reads the phone's crash reports; they stay on the phone. *Save reports* zips them for anyone helping you.
  - **Security**: passcode, iOS version, configuration profiles and device management, apps from outside the App Store, signs of a jailbreak. It says exactly what it looked at.
- **Photo clean-up** (Photos → *Clean up*): finds duplicates, similar shots, blurry or dark photos, old screenshots, large videos and images saved from chats. Tick what to keep a copy of; PhoneKeep saves them and checks every copy before it says they're safe. Removing them from the iPhone is up to you, in Photos (PhoneKeep shows the steps).
- **App lock**: Touch ID or your Mac's password (Windows Hello on Windows) before the phone window opens.
- **Photos**: Favourites, Albums, Videos, Screenshots, Live Photos and Hidden (which asks every time), then the library by year and month. Save as the original HEIC or as JPG, keeping the date taken and the location.
- **Quick preview everywhere**: press Space on a photo, video, PDF or file to see it, also straight from the iPhone. A photo that's only in iCloud shows the smaller version the phone keeps, marked *only in iCloud*.
- **Save everything** with one date range and one search.
- **Device overview**: model, colour, iOS, capacity, serial, **battery health** and **storage usage**.
- **Choose which iPhone** when several are reachable (cable and Wi-Fi); automatic backups can include more than one phone, one after the other.
- Smaller things: new app icons, trackpad scrolling on the Mac, a click on the Dock icon brings PhoneKeep's windows back, tidier main screen and photo chooser.

## After updating

- Nothing to do: your backups and settings carry over.
- The check-up fills in after your next backup (Stability and Security need the iPhone connected once).
- If you back up more than one iPhone, the phones you already back up stay ticked for automatic backups.

## Known limits

- **Installers are unsigned** (no Apple Developer ID yet; signing is planned for 5.2): on a Mac, right-click PhoneKeep → Open → Open the first time, or System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway. macOS asks once per executable to allow Keychain access; click *Always Allow*. Windows: if SmartScreen warns, click *More info → Run anyway*.
- Windows: untested on a device for this release (see above).
- **No restore to an iPhone yet** (planned for 5.2).
- Photo clean-up doesn't remove photos from the iPhone itself; that is planned for 5.2, after a safe way to do it is tested.
- No spyware check: it is planned for after 5.1.
- The Security card's passcode line says *not decided yet* until it has seen the iPhone locked with its passcode.
- With iCloud Photos on, most photos may be only in iCloud: PhoneKeep previews them from the smaller version the phone keeps, but can't save them, and clean-up leaves them out (it says how many).
- From 5.0, still true: a phone's newest backup exists twice on disk (about twice the phone's used storage for the first backup); not in a backup: saved passwords, Health, Screen Time; locked notes stay locked; PDFs show videos as a ▶ tile.
